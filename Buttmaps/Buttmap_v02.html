What this is

Place names that contain a butt-related term, compared across three US gazetteers. A gazetteer is a directory of place names with coordinates. Every place here matched a list of about 20 terms. The data were prepared in R as a shinydashboard and conveted to raw html using claude.

Data sources

SourceWho makes itWhat is in itThings to know
CensusUS Census Bureau, via the tigris R package Incorporated places (cities, towns, villages) and Census Designated Places, from the 2020 cartographic boundary files. Each place is placed at a point guaranteed to fall inside its boundary. Smallest of the three, and the most formal. These are populated places, so you will not find creeks, hills or lakes here.
GNISUS Geological Survey, for the US Board on Geographic Names The Domestic Names file: the official federal record of names for both cultural features (towns, schools, churches, parks) and physical ones (summits, streams, valleys, lakes). By far the largest source, with over two million features. It includes historical names and features that no longer exist. Public domain. USGS GNIS
OSMOpenStreetMap contributors, packaged by OSMNames Names from the OpenStreetMap planet file, US rows only. Roads, buildings and shops are removed; only place, natural, waterway and boundary features are kept. Crowdsourced, so it holds local and informal names the official sources lack, and the same real place is often listed more than once. Licensed under the ODbL.

Supporting data for the Counties tab

  • County boundaries: 2020 cartographic boundaries from the Census Bureau (tigris), simplified for the web. Each place is assigned to the county that contains it.
  • Population: 2020 decennial census total population (table P1_001N, tidycensus).
  • Place name totals: for each county, the total number of names of any kind in each gazetteer. This is the denominator for the "per 1,000 place names" measure, so a county is not ranked high just because it has lots of named features.

How it works

  • The three cards at the top are also the gazetteer checkboxes. Each shows the places displayed, after any filters, out of the total matching places in that gazetteer.
  • The Terms box starts with every term selected. Uncheck a term to drop it everywhere.
  • Populated places only keeps the Census places, GNIS populated places and Census designated places, and OSM cities, towns, villages and hamlets. This makes GNIS and OSM comparable to the Census by dropping the names of natural features.
  • The Terms filter and the Terms chart count each name once, under the first butt word found in it. The Names tab shows every butt word in a name and ranks names by how many they contain.
  • Repeats: the same real place can appear several times in one gazetteer, especially OSM. Rows with the same name within about 5 km are counted once. The map shows one point per place, merging the same place across the checked gazetteers.
  • Counties: the Counties tab colors each county by matching places per 1,000 place names in that county, per 100,000 residents, or as a raw count. Counties with very few names or people can swing widely.
